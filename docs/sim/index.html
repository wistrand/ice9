Model changes the run

Ice-nine, from Vonnegut's Cat's Cradle, melts at 45.8 °C, so the whole ocean is supercooled for it. A seed spreads by contact along the coasts and across the sea, and rain falling through air carrying its crystals seeds lakes, rivers and distant water. Each column freezes until its own latent heat warms it to the melting point, which happens at about two fifths of the ocean; after that the freeze goes only as fast as the air radiates heat to space. Whether the solid sinks (an open slush ocean near 42 °C) or floats (a crust, then a snowball) decides the ending. The constants are physical, not sped up; the sliders vary the ones nobody knows, such as the front speed or how much of a floating crust cracks open.

How the model works, where each constant comes from and what the runs show: see the project website and the paper (PDF).
