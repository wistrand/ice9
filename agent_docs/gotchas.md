## Traps

- **`file://` does not load the map.** `loadCells` uses `fetch`, which browsers block on `file://`. Always serve `docs/` over HTTP.
- **`'deflate'` in `DecompressionStream` means zlib (RFC 1950).** `cells.bin` is a zlib stream with header. `'deflate-raw'` would fail. If you regenerate the file, write it with `zlib.compress`, not raw deflate or gzip.
- **Front speed is calibrated, not emergent.** `p0` in `step` is scaled by `RES**0.15` and split over `np` passes so many small per-pass probabilities give the same km/h at any time step and grid. `rowProbs` then scales each neighbour's probability by its distance in km (east-west cells shrink with `cos(lat)`). Changing any of these changes the realised speed shown on the slider; re-measure if you touch them.
- **Fast-path skips in the heat loop assume rest state means no change.** Dormant water (all neighbours within 0.3 °C of their air-shifted target) and fully frozen interior cells skip the full update. If you add a new heat source or sink that acts on resting cells, it will be silently skipped unless you extend those conditions. The rest test must use the same `cp` coupling as the heat loop or cells never rest.
- **Counters are maintained incrementally.** `frozen`, `frozenMass`, `inlandFrozen`, `sheetFrozen` and `sysFrozen` are never recomputed from `phi` (except `frozenMass` and `massTotal` when the bathymetry checkbox toggles). Bypass `addPhi` and the stats drift.
- **Globe view renders at device pixel ratio 1.** `fit()` forces `dpr=1` in globe mode because `blitGlobe` does trigonometry per screen pixel. Raising it multiplies cost by up to 4.
- **Melting point by salinity: fresh water has none.** `meltPt(p, s)` returns `MELT` for `s = 0` (lakes, rivers) and clamps `1-p`; without that, a fully frozen lake computed 0/0 and turned the frozen share into NaN.
- **Satellite load is asynchronous.** `satImg.onload` calls `draw()` only when `base` loaded; the satellite path is skipped until `satPixels` is set.
- **The ice-sheet branch inside the generic melt cleanup is dead code.** In `step`, `kind===3` cells `continue` before the generic growth/melt block, so the `if(kind[i]===3)sheetFrozen-=...` there never runs. Harmless; left as in the original.

- **Chunk skipping must agree with the per-cell skip rules.** `chunkQuiet` in the heat loop encodes the same conditions as the dormant and resting-sheet tests. Change one without the other and chunks get frozen in time (or never skipped).
- **`grid` briefly holds 4 during frozen-list compaction.** Anything reading `grid` inside that loop sees it. It is restored before anything else runs.
- **Interior ice and east-west conduction are per tick, not per substep.** Both are exact or stable under that; anything else moved out of the substep loop needs the same argument.

- **`Td` is initialised in two places.** `build` ends with `T.set(T0);Td.set(T0d)` and `reset` does the same. Leaving one out starts the deep layer at 0 °C and it over-freezes by about 10% (found when the energy budget did not close).
- **A deep layer thinner than a few metres is no deep layer.** `initOceanColumn` zeroes `capd` below 1e-3; a residual of 1e-10 from `min(2.2,d/3700)-cap` once blew the vertical exchange up to Infinity. The exchange is implicit now, but keep the threshold.
- **Anything applied once per tick must be a rate, not an event.** Overturn used to mix the layers completely whenever the mixed layer was denser, once per tick, so the number of overturns grew as the time step shrank: the floating crust covered 84% of the ocean after two years at 48 h steps and 65% at 2 h. It now mixes `1 - exp(-h*OVERTURN_W/depth)` per step.
- **Rain carrying crystals seeds the sea as well as lakes.** The crystal field covers the oceans; seeding only inland water left the open sea to the front. With both, the first phase ends near day 20 instead of day 40.
- **Crystal sources are open water.** `crystalSrc` is weighted by `openSurface`: a closed crust lofts nothing.
- **Rain seeding lags one tick per round (open).** `vapourStep` and `snowStep` run once per tick, so each round of seeded sea, lofted crystals, rain and new seed waits a full tick. The sinking stall comes on day 18-20 at 48 h ticks, day 11 at 12 h, day 7 at 2 h, day 6 hourly. Values from day 30 on agree. The paper's timeline uses the 2 h runs for the first three years. Running both per substep would shorten the lag to the substep, not remove it.
- **`deepStep` runs every substep, not once per tick.** Its overturn test and deep freezing depend on the state, so calling it once per tick with the tick's length left the floating case step-dependent (35.5% vs 31.1% frozen at one year for 48 h vs 12 h ticks; now 30.7% vs 29.4%).
- **The deep layer freezes at the contact rate, not to equilibrium in one call.** `deepStep` once froze the deep layer to its melting point in a single call while overturning, so the crust grew at different rates for different time steps (74% vs 60% at two years for 48 h vs 2 h). Freezing is now limited to the share `contactF` of the deep water that reaches the crystals per step.
- **The initial ocean must be stable.** Polar surface water (−1 °C) is denser than the deep layer at 35 g/kg, so `initOceanColumn` lowers the mixed layer's initial salinity (`sal0`) just enough. Without that, every polar column overturns on the first tick. Overturn is now decided by density (`rhoW`), not by the old `Td > T0d + 1` rule.
- **`sal` is a bulk salinity.** The liquid's salinity is `sal/(1-phi)`. Overturn equalises liquid salinity, not bulk; mixing bulk values would create salt in the layer with more solid.
- **Latent heat depends on the temperature of freezing (Kirchhoff).** Solid ice-nine holds half the heat per degree of water, so `l(T) = l - 0.5*(MELT - T)`, with `l` measured at `MELT`. A column's enthalpy per unit capacity is `H = (1 - phi/2)*T - phi*(l - MELT/2)`; `latentStep` solves each freeze or melt increment at constant `H`, exactly, for the surface (`addPhi`) and the deep layer (`deepStep`). The step sizing (`warmingPerPhi`) uses the same `l(T)` so increments land on the moving melting point. Releasing a constant `l` (the earlier code) created `0.5*(MELT-T)` per unit frozen, 2-3.5% of all latent heat, and stalled the deep ocean at 0.40 instead of 0.43.
- **Every heat flow uses the heat capacity `cap*(1 - phi/2)`, not `cap`.** The surface loss handed to the air and to space, the current exchange (`exchange`), the floats deep exchange and the boil vent all take the solid's half heat capacity into account; interior ice uses 0.5. With `cap` alone the air received up to twice the heat the sea lost. Check with the energy budget: stores (`H` per column and layer, `C_AIR*airT`, snow latent heat, ice sheets) against the `budget` fluxes (sea surface to space, air to space, cap vent, seasonal input). It closes to 0.03% of the latent heat released over three years in both modes; run it with `tools/runs/ebhost.mjs` (see [tools/runs/README.md](../tools/runs/README.md)).

- **The air's radiative timescale was four times too long until the W/m² rewrite.** `AIR_K = 3e-4` per hour was documented as the 35-day Planck relaxation, which is 1.19e-3 per hour; every run before the greenhouse change (including the first paper) cooled the air to space four times too slowly. Radiation is now computed in W/m² over `C_AIR`, so the rate follows from the derived constants.
- **Albedo follows the white surface, not the solid fraction.** `frozen` sums solid fraction; in sinking mode a 36% slush column has open water on top. The albedo once used `frozen/waterCount` and so whitened slush; it now uses `seaWhite` from `updateRowSea`, the same surface rule as the map.
- **Split the sea's heat loss by the two fluxes, not by the coefficient ratio.** The loss is `ht*(u-dTa) + H_LW*u`; `airShare` gives the air the first term's share. Using `ht/hx` instead sent the longwave part into the air and the air ran 5 K too hot.
- **The old defaults compressed time by 100–1000×.** Per-column "cooling to space" was 17,000 W/m²/K equivalent (physical: 40–100), the air relaxed in 0.7 h (physical: 35 days), the albedo floor was −8 K (physical: −35). With derived constants a run is weeks of action then decades of creep; do not "fix" that by raising rates. See [physics.md](physics.md).

- **The air anomaly is measured against the local climatology `airTclim`.** Before `airTclim` the air was a uniform 14 °C field and the sea used `airT - TA0`. Mixing and wind are linear, so the anomaly evolves the same either way and the sea forcing was the same (seeded 1.8-year runs agree to 0.01% frozen and 0.1 K air in both modes); what was wrong was the absolute air field (tropics too cold, poles too warm), which shows in the air overlay and the `T³` Planck factor. Do not read the hot coastal cells in floats mode as a bug: open tropical water whose climatology (28 °C) plus `cp` times the local air anomaly (+15 to +20 K in the tropics) exceeds 43.6 °C cannot freeze, and those are the last tropical waters the front reaches.
- **A mixed sinking column is measured against today's sea surface, not its own mean.** `T0` for a sinking column is its depth mean (2-8 °C). Once seeded the column is slush top to bottom, so its surface is its temperature; measuring 43 °C slush against 8 °C counted a 35 K anomaly where the surface anomaly was 17 K, and the air settled 15-20 K above the sea (60 °C tropics). `seaRef` switches the reference; the rest test, both relaxation branches and `airShare` must all use it. Seeded lakes and rivers use the annual air climatology (`airTannual`) for the same reason; their `T0` is the column-mean formula (about 5 °C) and made them lose heat too fast.
- **Anomaly transport can push air above the sea it sits over.** Mixing moves the warming, not the heat down the absolute gradient, so air from latitudes that warmed more lifts the tropics above their sea. The convective cap (`CONV_DT`, `updateRowSea`) bounds that. It caps warming, not temperature: `rowSea` is the mean of `T - seaRef(i)` (the surface's departure from its own climatology), added to each air cell's `airTclim`. Capping the absolute temperature at sea + 3 K pinned continental summers at about 45 °C and kept snow everywhere.
- **The air climatology moves with the date.** `setAirSeason(true)` adds the climatology's change to `airT` so anomalies survive; `reset` and `build` call it with `false` after setting the date, then copy `airTclim` into `airT`. Copying first starts the air on the wrong season.
- **Land rain is today's map plus the extra vapour, not today's map times a ratio.** Scaling by `vapour/vapourToday` gave land rain at 55-70x today's while evaporation was 3x: where the reference field is small, a ratio of small numbers explodes. The additive anomaly conserves water. The ratio is used only for less vapour than today, where it is bounded by 1.
- **Air transport must be stable at any step, not capped.** The old explicit air scheme capped each substep at 0.45 cells, about 1 m/s of wind, so heat, vapour and crystals reached about 160 km inland in a vapour lifetime and the tropics warmed by only half the polar anomaly. `transportAir` now shifts each row by semi-Lagrangian interpolation and mixes implicitly per row and column with the sphere's metric; it conserves the area-weighted total to 1e-9 and is faster than the explicit version it replaced. An explicit multi-pass version at the same rates gave the same results (air 49.3 against 49.4 °C at day 80) at 1.4x the cost.
- **`snowStep` runs inside `step`, before `airStep`, on the last substep only.** `airIn` is cleared at the start of each substep, so latent heat from freezing rain added after `airStep` would be lost; it is accumulated over the whole tick (`h*last`), as is `evapQ`.
- **Heat transport by currents must conserve heat.** `advect` is a symmetric exchange between a column and its downstream neighbour (`exchange`), done in a pre-pass into `advHeat` and applied in the heat loop; a cell with non-zero `advHeat` is never treated as at rest. Do not replace it with upwind advection: the velocity field is not divergence-free on the grid, and the advective form destroyed 1.6× the heat the surface shed by year 3 while the flux form piled heat into convergence cells. Advecting the anomaly from climatology was also tried: it stirs a uniform slush ocean with a transport that no longer exists.

## Findings

### Gyre shapes in the freeze after years

- **Symptom:** after several simulated years under derived constants, the locked regions traced the gyre stream functions, with a hot streak off Peru in one variant.
- **Diagnosis:** the upwind current advection was not heat-conserving on the model's divergent discrete flow; the fast per-column cooling of the old constants had hidden it. Measured: heat destroyed by advection exceeded the heat shed through the surface by 1.6× at year 3.
- **Fix:** conservative along-flow exchange (`exchange`), verified to conserve to 1e-6 of the shed heat. The freeze after the adiabatic third slowed from 54% to 41% at year 8, which is what the radiation budget predicts.
- **Takeaway:** any term that moves heat between columns must be written as an exchange whose two sides sum to zero in `cap*AREA` units. Measure conservation before trusting a multi-year run.

### Split from a single-file artifact

- **Origin:** the app was a single 510 KB HTML artifact (since removed from the repo) with the cell mask and satellite JPEG inlined as base64 and pako loaded from a CDN.
- **Change:** split into `index.html`, `style.css`, `app.js` and binary assets in `docs/sim/data/`; pako replaced by `DecompressionStream`; the IIFE became `async` so it can `await loadCells()` before anything else runs.
- **Takeaway:** the original is no longer in the repo; the model has since diverged from it on purpose (see [physics.md](physics.md)). `docs/sim/data/` was rebuilt by `tools/build_data.py` and differs slightly from the original data; see [data.md](data.md).
